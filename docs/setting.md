# Setting

The world the mechanics are dressed in, the vocabulary we use for it, and the
rules we hold ourselves to when borrowing from a living tradition.

Mechanics live in `docs/mechanics.md`; this file is what things are called and
why.

## The anchor

**Te reo Māori and Māori cosmology**, not "Polynesian" generally. Polynesia is
many peoples with distinct traditions, and blending Māori, Hawaiian, Samoan and
Tahitian material into one aesthetic is exactly what makes a setting read as
pastiche. One tradition, held consistently, is both better craft and better
manners. The title is te reo, so that is the anchor.

## Where you are

A **pā** — a fortified village — on a shore between the forest and the sea. Its
people garden, fish, cut timber, quarry stone, and keep the rites. You are its
**rangatira**: you decide who does what, what gets built, and what the pā does
while you are not watching.

## What has gone wrong

The cosmology sits in the background, as background: Ranginui and Papatūānuku
separated by their children; **Tangaroa** holding the sea; **Tāne** holding the
forest; the old grievance between them, when the children of Tangaroa were
scattered and some took shelter inland rather than return to the water.

Something has disturbed the balance between them. The sea is taking back what
it lost, creature by creature, up the beach — and a pā on the shore is standing
exactly where that argument is being settled.

Your own **kaitiaki** — the guardian of this stretch of coast, this pā's own,
not one of the great atua — has gone silent. Nothing is holding the line. The
work of the tohunga, and of the branch of the tree they serve, is to find out
why, and to be heard again.

The kaitiaki is **fictional and specific to this game**. That is deliberate: it
gives us something to build mechanics on without turning a real atua into a
stat block, an enemy, or a vending machine.

## How we handle it

Rules for anyone writing text for this game, including future me.

1. **The great atua are cosmology, not content.** Tangaroa, Tāne and the rest
   are the weather the story happens in. They are never enemies, never bosses,
   never purchasable, and never speak in the UI. The being the player deals
   with mechanically is the invented kaitiaki.
2. **No invented karakia.** Ritual language is real, often tapu, and not ours
   to fabricate. We say that the tohunga keep the rites; we do not script them
   or print words as though they were real ones.
3. **Nothing iwi-specific.** We stay with the widely published general
   cosmology. Local and iwi traditions are not ours to retell.
4. **Macrons, always.** Pā, tohunga, kūmara, Tāne, Papatūānuku. Correct
   spelling is the floor, not an extra.
5. **Restraint in the UI.** A small set of nouns the player learns by using
   them — pā, rangatira, toa, tohunga, pātaka, mana — and plain English for
   everything else. Sprinkling te reo over every label reads as costume and
   multiplies the chances of getting something wrong.
6. **The glossary is a best effort, not an authority.** Terms below marked
   _verify_ are my understanding and want checking against a real dictionary
   (Te Aka is the standard one) before they reach the screen.
7. **Get a speaker to read it.** Before this is public in any real sense, the
   text wants review by someone with actual standing to give it. Nothing in
   here substitutes for that.

| **toa**        | Warrior                                       | The warrior job                  |

| **mana**       | Standing, authority, spiritual weight         | The monotonic progression gate   |

Terms I have deliberately **not** committed to, because I am not confident
enough in them to put them on screen: the specific words for a palisade, a
rampart, and the parts of a pā's defenses. The wall is "the wall" until someone
who knows tells us better.

## The two currencies

The atua branch runs on two numbers, and which word goes on which matters.

- **Devotion** — the spendable balance, generated by tohunga keeping the
  karakia. Spent on the permanent blessings in the branch, and on nothing else.
- **Mana** — the monotonic total, which only ever grows, and gates which tier
  of the branch is open at all.

Mana is the gate rather than the currency on purpose. Mana is standing: it is
held and grown through deeds, not spent like coins at a stall. Making it the
number that never goes down is both the mechanically useful choice — a player
who spends freely reaches the same tiers as one who hoards — and the one that
does not flatten the word into magic points, which is what forty years of
fantasy games have done to it.

## Naming

**Mate Atua.** Two readings I can see, and both suit the game: an affliction of
divine origin — a sickness sent, which is what the shore is suffering — or a
god that has died, which is what the silence of the kaitiaki looks like from
inside the pā. If the intended sense is the second, word order is worth
checking with a speaker; te reo generally puts the modifier after the noun, so
_Atua Mate_ may be the phrasing that lands.
